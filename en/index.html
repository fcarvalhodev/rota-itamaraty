Who owns strategic infrastructure

It is not only China. Companies owned by foreign governments, or with a government as a major shareholder, have bought power plants, transmission lines, distributors, gas pipelines, a refinery, oil fields and port terminals in Brazil. Most of the time, the seller was the Brazilian government itself, through auctions or sales by Petrobras and Eletrobras.

The US does not appear on this list because it has no state companies of this kind here: the American presence comes from private companies and, since 2026, from financing by the DFC agency. The list shows the largest deals we found with a source, not all of them. Before that, in the 1990s, privatizations had already handed distributors to foreign groups, such as Eletropaulo in 1998.

What stays in Brazil and what goes back abroad

Even when a foreign company invests in its own subsidiary, part of the money stays here: wages, taxes, purchases from suppliers and the factory itself. What leaves are the profits sent to the parent company. In 2025, Brazil sent abroad a net US$81.3 billion in profits and interest, and received US$77.6 billion in direct investment. How each one negotiates

    “The US negotiates with threats, China with offers”

    On social media the phrase appears as “the US negotiates at gunpoint”, meaning with threats. We listed each one's threats and offers to Brazil since 2003.

    US

    Threats and punishments
    • 2020: the ambassador says Brazil will face “consequences” if it lets Huawei into 5G.
    • 2025: Trump threatens an extra tariff on anyone who aligns with BRICS, during the summit in Rio.
    • 2025: a 50% tariff with a letter citing Bolsonaro's trial, and a Magnitsky sanction against Moraes.
    • 2026: CV and PCC designated as terrorists, against the request of Lula, who feared sanctions on banks and military action.
    • 2026: a new 25% tariff and a 12.5% surcharge, citing even Pix.
    Offers and agreements
    • 2009–2014: after losing to Brazil at the WTO in the cotton case, the US pays US$147 million a year and another US$300 million in the final settlement.
    • 2019: support for joining the OECD, in exchange for Brazil giving up special treatment at the WTO, and major non-NATO ally status.
    • 2020: a fund to finance Ericsson and Nokia 5G instead of Huawei.
    • 2025–2026: a rollback of beef and coffee tariffs after negotiation, and US$565 million for rare earths.

    China

    Threats and punishments
    • 2020: the ambassador calls Eduardo Bolsonaro's remark a “malicious insult” and demands an apology.
    • 2021: delays in CoronaVac vaccine inputs. Butantan linked the delay to Bolsonaro's attacks; the embassy denied it and the Health Ministry said it was a contract issue. It was never proven to be retaliation.
    • 2025–2026: a beef safeguard with a quota and surcharge, applied to all major exporters.
    • Outside Brazil: economic punishment of Australia and Lithuania after political disagreements.
    Offers and agreements
    • 2004: ten agreements and a promise of US$10 billion in investment when Brazil recognizes China as a market economy.
    • 2014: the New Development Bank, created by BRICS in Fortaleza.
    • 2023 and 2024: 15 agreements on Lula's visit to Beijing and 37 on Xi's visit to Brasília.
    • 2025: R$27 billion in announcements and Brazil as the top destination for Chinese investment in the world.
    Half true

    In the episodes selected on this page, explicit political pressure comes mainly from Washington, several with political demands, from 2020 to 2026. China's was a harsh verbal reaction and a vaccine episode never proven to be retaliation.

    Both halves of the phrase have caveats. The US also makes offers and paid when it lost at the WTO. China has punished other countries economically and uses its weight as the largest buyer, as the beef quota showed. Outside Brazil, the US acted militarily in Venezuela in 2026 and supported the 1964 coup with Operation Brother Sam.

    Is it true?

    The most repeated claims from both sides.

    Verdict

    Neither domination nor partnership without interest. Brazil depends on the two powers in different ways.

    • China: buys almost 30% of what Brazil exports and Brazil has a surplus with it. It invests mainly in energy and oil, mostly by buying companies that already existed. The risk is concentration: a buyer that big weighs on prices, as the beef quota showed.
    • US: still the largest foreign investor, with about a quarter of the stock, in industry, finance and technology. This page's selection includes US pressure with political conditions between 2020 and 2026; it is not an exhaustive inventory of all diplomatic relations.
    • Return for the people: wages, purchases, taxes and remittances have different effects. The available data do not allow comparing total jobs at all American and Chinese companies. Acquisitions carry historical weight in the CEBC survey; greenfield projects gained share in 2025, but already existed before.
    • Strategic assets: the table brings together operations by state companies from China and from Italy, France, Norway, the Emirates, Canada and Singapore. It includes stakes, acquisitions and concessions carried out under different Brazilian governments, with their own rules. The September 2026 critical minerals policy added control instruments; mining and investments were already subject to earlier regulation.

    The useful question is not which power is good, but how much Brazil depends on each and what rules it has for negotiating with both.
